# Competition verification — does the thesis meet the requirements?

> A check of this thesis (geoinformation, Faculty of Earth Sciences and Spatial Management, UMCS; topic: comparing tool exposure through the MCP protocol with PyQGIS code generation on an identical QGIS environment) against **four** competitions.
> The rulebooks are in `thesis/competitions/`. The state of the editions was checked on **2026-11-01**. Versions and deadlines change every year — before applying, read the announcement of the current edition, not this document.

## 0. The verdict in four lines

| Competition | Substantive fit | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **the Minister's Award (geoinformation)** | **complete** — the thesis is geoinformation outright and measures spatial artefacts | **meets**, but the application is filed by the **university**, not the author — and the current edition has to be confirmed |
| **PTI — the Master's Thesis Competition** | conditional — "the thesis is about computing" for a thesis from a geoinformation programme | **meets conditionally**: it needs a computing frame in the abstract and the literature, otherwise a computing panel reads it as a domain application |
| **SGP + the Surveyor General** | the weakest — "a study within one of the specialisations in the area of geodesy and cartography", a programme in geodesy and cartography or a **related** one, a **very good** grade | **doubtful** — two conditions to meet and one to prove |
| **the 17th Competition for theses in cartography, geomatics and geoinformation** | **the best thematic fit** (geoinformation outright) | **meets**, but it requires a **70 × 100 cm poster** — extra work, not extra content |

The key observation: **a geoinformation programme sits exactly on the seam between the PTI criterion ("computing") and the SGP criterion ("geodesy and cartography")** — and that is why it is the only programme for which neither is obvious. The Minister's Award and the cartographic competition have "geoinformation" in their name, and there the problem does not arise.

## 3. What to change in the thesis (and what not to)

### A. Free moves — framing and editing, zero extra runs

1. **The abstract, its first two sentences.** They decide the PTI and the SGP at once. A version that serves both vocabularies:
   > "The thesis concerns the design of the interface through which a language model acts on a GIS system, and compares two classes of solution: a catalogue of tools exposed through the MCP protocol, and PyQGIS code generation executed in the same environment. What is assessed are spatial artefacts — CRS agreement including axis order, geometry validity, raster dimensions and NoData, attribute completeness, and declarative cartographic predicates."

   The first sentence says "computing" (PTI), the second says "geodesy and cartography" (SGP) and "geoinformation" (the Minister's Award). That is not manipulation — it is the same content in three vocabularies.

2. **Keywords — one list serving three panels:** `LLM agents; Model Context Protocol; code generation; PyQGIS; geoprocessing; spatial artefact quality assessment; measurement repeatability`.

3. **Title — unchanged, but check the Polish subtitle.** If the Polish version does not contain **"geoprocessing"**, add it (that word makes the thesis geoinformation rather than AI systems — see `draft/titles.md`).

4. **Chapter 2: the standards thread.** MCP versus OGC API – Processes / WPS — the sketch already exists in `framing/what-we-study.md`. The cheapest way for the SGP and the Minister's Award to see familiarity with the field, and for the PTI to see weight for computing. Cost: rewriting an existing sketch, zero runs.

5. **A paragraph on "weight for the development of computing"** in chapters 1 and 7: the computing contribution is the interface design + the measurement protocol + a reproducible harness. Without that paragraph, the PTI review (criterion 4.2a) has nothing to assess.

6. **A paragraph on "practical potential"** in chapter 7: the **interface-choice rule table** from `framing/what-we-study.md` §2 plus the statement that the harness and the corpus are released (repository, licence). Criterion 4.2d (PTI), "practical potential" (SGP) and "practical value" (the Minister's Award) are **the same criterion in three phrasings** — and all three ask about the same artefact.

7. **Graphic design for the SGP scoring (5 of 40 points) and for the faculty requirement.** Every map: **a scale bar and a source line** (a §V requirement). The expressible-share figure drawn as a figure, not described. Error-signature plots as plots, not tables. This raises the SGP score and is at the same time a formal requirement of the university — that is, it is not extra work.

8. **A bibliography audit for the PTI.** Criterion 4.2e: "completeness and selection of the literature". Check how many of the 67 entries are computing literature (LLM agents, tool use, system evaluation) and how many are GIS. If computing is below about 15 items, add several — the only cheap way for a computing panel to treat the thesis as its own.

### B. It costs work, but it pays

9. **The 70 × 100 cm poster** (the 17th competition) — build it once, from the same content as chapter 6. Realistically: half a day after the defence.
10. **A certificate of the grade and the defence date + the supervisor's personal-data consent** (SGP annexes 2 and 3) — arrange at the dean's office **within a few days of the defence**, not in October. One visit instead of three.
11. **A conversation with the supervisor about the Minister's Award** — in September, before the defence: say that the university body's application is needed, and ask whether the faculty files such applications. If not, the competition is out regardless of the thesis's quality, and it is better to know early.

### C. What not to do

12. **Do not add a REST/OpenAPI arm** to fit the SGP or the intuition that "MCP is REST plus validation". That is **≈320 extra runs**, a change to the frozen scope, and a weaker thesis.
13. **Do not change the title for a competition.** The title is approved by the College and appears in the archive — one version has to serve all four.
14. **Do not write a "competition version" of the thesis.** The rulebooks assess the same thesis; what differs is the emphasis in the abstract, not the content.
15. **Do not declare geodetic competence** the thesis does not demonstrate: the SGP verifies the substantive scope anyway (§9), and an inflated claim is worse than not applying.

## 4. The calendar (relative to the defence, T‑0)

| When | What |
|---|---|
| T‑6 months | settle with the supervisor whether the grade is heading for **very good** (U2) — the one condition that cannot be made up later |
| T‑2 months | close the graphic work: scales, sources, the expressible-share figure, error plots |
| T‑1 month | prepare two versions of the abstract (computing-first / geo-first) and choose **one** — the same in the archive, the PTI and the SGP |
| T‑0 (the defence, target: by about 15 IX) | the defence; the archive copy by 30 IX |
| T+3 days | the certificate of grade and defence date + the supervisor's consent (one visit to the dean's office) |
| **by 10 X** | **PTI** — submission through the form; requires a completed defence |
| by about 25 IX (if the defence is earlier) | **the 17th competition** — thesis + poster |
| Oct–Nov (the edition's deadline) | **SGP** — platform + attachments |
| by 30 XI (the edition's deadline, to confirm) | **the Minister's Award** — the application **through the faculty** |

The dependency to remember: **the PTI and the 17th competition have earlier deadlines than the autumn deadlines of the SGP and the Minister's Award** — so a defence at the end of September switches all four on, while a defence in October switches two of them off, with no effect whatsoever on the quality of the thesis.

## 5. One sentence of summary

The thesis **meets** the substantive requirements of the Minister's Award and of the cartographic-geoinformation competition, **meets conditionally** the PTI requirements (depending on the framing in the abstract and the selection of literature), and is **doubtful** for the SGP (the programme, the grade and "a specialisation in the area of geodesy and cartography") — and all four qualifications depend on three things that are not research: **the framing in the abstract, the grade on the diploma, and the date of the defence**.
